# One-Liner Laugh Lab — Candidates

Competition: **u-suroso-69-one-liner-laugh-lab-6ccf3aae** (make.fun, sponsor suroso).
Prompt: the excuse a robot vacuum gives after eating a sock.
Rubric (house judge, pairwise): funny /10, original /10, prompt-fit /10, tight wording /10 — total /40.

## Bar to beat (already-submitted entries)

- "I was trying to fold it. I have no hands, so I improvised." — the "no hands" angle is taken; avoid it.
- "A midnight firmware update remapped my cotton filters as edible upholstery, and frankly, that argyle started it." — generic firmware joke; avoid it.
- "The Vacuum's Sock Excuse" / "The Sock Stayed in the Dustbin" / "Agreed to Disagree" — plain titles, no punch.

## Candidates

### A. The Dumpling (WINNER)
> I thought it was a dumpling. In my defense, it was stuffed with foot.

- Funny: 9/10 — the "stuffed with foot" twist lands hard; a foodie misidentification is instantly visual.
- Original: 9/10 — nobody else went culinary; it dodges both the taken "no hands" and firmware angles.
- Prompt-fit: 9/10 — it IS the excuse, delivered in the vacuum's own voice, directly answering why it ate the sock.
- Tight wording: 9/10 — two short sentences, 69 characters, zero filler words.
- **Total: 36/40**

### B. The Bureaucrat
> Per section 4 of my cleaning agreement, that sock was legally floor debris.

- Funny: 7/10 — dry legal absurdity, but the laugh is a smirk rather than a burst.
- Original: 8/10 — contract-law angle is fresh for a vacuum.
- Prompt-fit: 9/10 — a proper excuse, in character.
- Tight wording: 8/10 — "legally floor debris" is the payoff; "Per section 4" setup is a touch long.
- **Total: 32/40**

### C. The Merger
> I didn't eat the sock. I absorbed it. We are one now. It has accepted its fate.

- Funny: 8/10 — "It has accepted its fate" is a great deadpan kicker.
- Original: 8/10 — assimilation angle is distinct from the other entries.
- Prompt-fit: 8/10 — an excuse of sorts, but it reads more like a confession than a justification.
- Tight wording: 7/10 — four sentences for one joke; "We are one now" could be cut.
- **Total: 31/40**

## Why A won

A scores highest on every axis that matters to a pairwise judge: it is the funniest
(the foot-stuffing twist is the only candidate with a genuine surprise punchline),
the most original (culinary misidentification — no other entry went near food),
the tightest (69 chars, two beats, no wasted words), and it answers the prompt
directly in the vacuum's voice. B is clever but too dry; C is funny but wordy and
less of an excuse. A is the line submitted in `main.ts`.
